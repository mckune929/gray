# Product Spec — PMP 8-Week Domain-Weighted Study Planner

**Spec version:** 2026-05-16 · **Baseline:** PMP Study Product Competitive Baseline (researched 2026-05-16) · **Chosen gap:** Section 6, Gap #1 (domain-weighted 8-week intensive), shipped at Entry-tier scope (planner only).

---

## 1. Product identity

- **Product ID:** `pmp-planner-8wk-v1`
- **Working title:** PMP 8-Week Domain-Weighted Study Planner
- **One-line description:** An 8-week PMP study planner that allocates study hours by PMI's 42/50/8 domain weights.
- **Niche cluster:** PMP certification prep
- **Position in cluster:** Anchor entry SKU. This planner is the differentiated core of the cluster and ships first as a standalone product. A formula reference and a practice-question bank are planned as separate later SKUs that can be cross-sold and bundled with this planner off the same production cycle.

## 2. Target buyer

- **Primary buyer profile:** A working professional in their 30s or 40s, employed full-time, often with a family, prepping for the PMP exam on a self-study basis over a compressed 8-week window. They have already completed the mandatory 35-hour contact course (a $300–$2,000+ spend), so they are not paying for content instruction — they are paying for *structure* and *confidence*. They are a first-time taker with acute first-try anxiety: a fail costs $275+ and a 30-day cooling-off period. They are active on r/pmp and LinkedIn PMP communities and read reviews closely before buying.
- **What they've tried that hasn't worked:** Free Notion PMP trackers (too basic — a knowledge-area checklist, no allocation logic); generic time-based study planners (treat all content equally, so they over-study the 8% Business domain and under-prepare the 50% Process domain); AI-written KDP prep books (reviewers call them "AI drivel," cite repeated questions, poor explanations, and no visuals).
- **5-star review they would leave:** *"I stopped guessing where to spend my evenings — the hours were already mapped to what the exam actually tests, and I walked in with nothing surprising me."*

## 3. Problem and outcome

- **Problem statement:** Self-study PMP candidates have a fixed, scarce number of evening and weekend hours and no reliable way to know where to spend them. Generic planners are organized by calendar or by book chapter, treating all material as equally weighted. The result is misallocated effort — disproportionate time on the 8% Business Environment domain, not enough on the 50% Process domain — and a candidate who walks into a high-stakes exam unsure whether their prep matched the test.
- **Stated outcome:** The buyer completes a structured 120-hour, 8-week prep cycle in which study time is allocated to PMI's published domain weights, tracks readiness per domain against PMI's Above Target / Target / Below Target / Needs Improvement rating language, and enters the exam having scheduled and debriefed three full-length practice exams.
- **Why existing alternatives fall short:**
  - No existing Etsy listing allocates study hours by PMI's published 42/50/8 domain weights — planners are time-based, not weight-based.
  - Free Notion trackers set a quality floor but offer no allocation logic or readiness scoring.
  - AI-generated KDP books are flooding the category and visibly failing on duplicate questions, weak explanations, and absent visuals.

## 4. Positioning and differentiation

- **The single differentiator:** Every study hour is allocated to PMI's published 42/50/8 domain weights, so prep time mirrors the actual exam.
- **Three supporting differentiators:**
  1. Progress is tracked as domain readiness (People / Process / Business) using PMI's own Above Target / Target rating language — not as calendar pages checked off.
  2. The 120-hour plan is internally hour-verified: domain totals, weekly totals, and the grand total reconcile exactly, with the math shown to the buyer.
  3. Human-authored, sourced to the current PMI Examination Content Outline, and built with real visuals (domain pie chart, weekly grids) — a direct answer to the AI-book backlash.
- **What this product is explicitly NOT:**
  - Not a content course or textbook. It assumes the buyer's 35-hour course taught the material; this planner structures and schedules it.
  - Not a practice-question bank or formula sheet. Those are planned as separate companion SKUs; this product is the schedule-and-track layer only.
  - Not a Notion app or editable template. v1 is print-ready PDF only; a Notion port is a planned later product.

## 5. Format and deliverables

- **Primary file format:** PDF only (one file).
- **Page count / unit:** Approximately 52 pages, US Letter (8.5×11 in) portrait, 0.75-inch margins.
- **Design constraints:** Print-ready. Color, but every page must remain legible printed in grayscale (no information conveyed by color alone). Headings: Archivo (SIL Open Font License). Body: Source Sans 3 (SIL Open Font License) at 11pt — both free for commercial use, so there is no font-licensing exposure. Typographic hierarchy: H1 24pt bold / H2 16pt semibold / H3 13pt semibold / body 11pt regular / captions 9pt. All text is real selectable text, not flattened images; minimum body contrast meets WCAG AA.
- **Deliverable folder contents when complete:**
  - `pmp-8wk-planner-2026.pdf`

## 6. Content structure

### Master hour allocation (drives the whole planner — verify before building any page)

Total prep: **120 hours over 8 weeks = 15 hours/week.**

| Bucket | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| People domain study & drilling | 42 | Weeks 1–3 |
| Process domain study & drilling | 50 | Weeks 4–7 |
| Business Environment domain study & drilling | 8 | Weeks 7–8 |
| Orientation | 3 | Week 1 |
| Full-length mock exams (3 × 4 h) | 12 | Weeks 5, 6, 8 |
| Final review & exam-day prep | 5 | Week 8 |
| **TOTAL** | **120** | |

Domain hours are literally 42 / 50 / 8 — the weighting is the number, not an approximation. Weekly skeleton (each row sums to 15): Wk1 = Orientation 3 + People 12; Wk2 = People 15; Wk3 = People 15; Wk4 = Process 15; Wk5 = Process 11 + Mock 4; Wk6 = Process 11 + Mock 4; Wk7 = Process 13 + Business 2; Wk8 = Business 6 + Mock 4 + Review 5.

### 8-Week Domain-Weighted Planner (≈ 52 pages)

1. **Cover page** (1 p) — Title, "Built to the 2026 PMI Examination Content Outline," version date.
2. **How to use this planner** (2 pp) — The 120-hour model, the 15-hour week, recommended weekday/weekend split (e.g., 2 h × 5 weekdays + 5 h weekend = 15), and a note that the buyer slots their own full-length practice exam into each scheduled mock.
3. **Domain weighting explainer** (1 p) — *Required:* a 42/50/8 pie chart and one paragraph sourcing the weights to the PMI ECO.
4. **Readiness tracking system** (1 p) — Explains the per-domain Above Target / Target / Below Target / Needs Improvement self-rating mirroring PMI's performance ratings.
5. **Master hour allocation table** (1 p) — Reproduces the table above so the buyer sees the math reconcile.
6. **8 weekly modules** (≈ 40 pp, 5 pp each) — Each module contains: weekly focus and domain(s); that week's domain hour count; a 7-day grid with daily hour targets summing to 15; a task checklist; a mini domain-readiness check.
7. **3 mock-exam debrief sheets** (3 pp) — One per scheduled mock: score capture, per-domain breakdown, weakest-domain remediation prompt.
8. **Final review & exam-day toolkit** (2 pp) — Exam-day checklist, brain-dump sheet, what-to-expect summary (180 questions, 230 minutes, question types).
9. **Cumulative domain-readiness scorecard** (1 p) — Tracks People / Process / Business readiness across all 8 weeks in one view.

- **Source material:** PMI Examination Content Outline (current edition); competitive baseline Sections 1 and 5.
- **Internal consistency requirements:** Each weekly grid's daily targets sum to 15; weekly totals sum to 120; domain hours across all weeks sum to exactly 42 / 50 / 8; the pie chart, the master table, and the per-week labels all show the same numbers; the cumulative scorecard's domain rows match the domains used in every weekly mini-readiness check.

- **Tier:** Entry ($9–15 window from the baseline). $12 sits mid-window — above the sub-$9 threshold the baseline warns signals low quality, below the $15 ceiling.
- **Bundle role:** Stands alone. It is the anchor of the cluster; planned companion SKUs (formula reference, practice-question bank) will later cross-sell and bundle with it, but this product is complete and saleable on its own.

## 11. Production notes

- **Skills to load:** `pdf/SKILL.md` before starting.
- **External data the Production Agent must NOT invent:** Domain weights (People 42% / Process 50% / Business 8%); exam structure (180 questions, 230 minutes, 5 unscored pre-test items); question types; performance-rating labels (Above Target / Target / Below Target / Needs Improvement). All of these must be confirmed against the actual **PMI Examination Content Outline** before use — the baseline reports them but the official ECO PDF was not supplied to this spec (see "needs validation" below). Do not state a numeric passing score; PMI publishes none.
- **Known production risks:**
  1. *Hour math errors.* Calculate domain and weekly totals first (42/50/8 → weeks → 120), then divide into daily targets — never calculate days independently and hope they sum.
  2. *Domain-weighting must be visible fast.* If a buyer flipping pages 1–5 can't see the 42/50/8 split, the differentiator is invisible and the product fails its own positioning.
  3. *Shelf-life.* PMI is expected to update the ECO (AI/sustainability topics) in July 2026. Keep all version dating explicit and avoid claims that the July update would contradict.
  4. *Scope discipline.* This product schedules mock exams but does not supply questions or formulas. Do not add a question set or formula pages — those are separate planned SKUs, and including them here cannibalizes them and breaks the Entry-tier price.

## 12. Success criteria
